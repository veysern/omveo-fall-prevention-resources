# Fall Prevention Checklist

A practical, evidence-based guide for reducing fall risk in older adults and care settings.
Based on public-domain CDC STEADI (Stopping Elderly Accidents, Deaths & Injuries) guidelines.

---

## Home Environment

- **Lighting** — Ensure all stairways, hallways, and bathrooms have bright, working light bulbs with switches accessible at both ends.
- **Trip hazards** — Remove loose rugs, power cords, and clutter from all walking paths throughout the home.
- **Non-slip surfaces** — Place non-slip mats or adhesive strips in the bathtub, shower, and on bare-floor areas.
- **Grab bars** — Install sturdy grab bars beside the toilet and inside the shower or tub, anchored to wall studs.
- **Stair safety** — Confirm that handrails are present on both sides of every staircase and are firmly secured.
- **Furniture layout** — Arrange furniture to create clear, wide walking paths of at least 36 inches throughout living areas.
- **Outdoor paths** — Keep walkways, steps, and driveways free of ice, leaves, and uneven surfaces year-round.

---

## Personal Health & Medication

- **Medication review** — Have a healthcare provider review all medications, including over-the-counter drugs, at least once a year for fall-risk side effects.
- **Vision check** — Get an eye examination annually and update glasses prescriptions to maintain accurate depth perception.
- **Footwear** — Wear properly fitting shoes or slippers with non-slip soles and low heels inside and outside the home.
- **Orthostatic hypotension** — Rise slowly from sitting or lying positions and pause for a few seconds before walking to prevent dizziness.
- **Hydration** — Drink adequate fluids daily, as dehydration can cause lightheadedness and increase fall risk.

---

## Physical Conditioning

- **Balance exercises** — Perform a structured balance and strength exercise program (e.g., Tai Chi, Otago) at least three times per week.
- **Assistive devices** — Use a prescribed cane, walker, or other mobility aid consistently whenever moving, especially on uneven terrain.
- **Strength training** — Include lower-body strength exercises at least twice per week to maintain leg muscle mass and stability.

---

## Care & Monitoring

- **Fall history** — Report any fall or near-fall to a healthcare provider promptly to assess underlying causes and update the care plan.
- **Emergency communication** — Keep a phone, medical alert device, or call system within reach at all times, especially when alone.
- **Caregiver awareness** — Ensure caregivers and family members are trained to recognize fall risk factors and apply prevention strategies consistently.

---

*Sources: CDC STEADI Initiative (public domain), National Institute on Aging fall prevention resources.*
